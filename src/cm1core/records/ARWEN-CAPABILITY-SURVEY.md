# What the pinned ArWen estate makes reachable

Companion prose for `ARWEN-CAPABILITY-SURVEY.json`, which is generated by
`tools/survey_arwen_capabilities.py` directly from the pinned estate and is
the authority. Regenerate it, do not hand-edit it:

```text
python3 tools/survey_arwen_capabilities.py \
    --estate-root tmp/arwen-estate-gpuwm-2.8.9 \
    --baseline-root tmp/arwen-estate-gpuwm-2.8.0 \
    --output src/cm1core/records/ARWEN-CAPABILITY-SURVEY.json
```

The survey is CPU-only. It imports neither `gpuwm` nor CuPy and opens no CUDA
context: every row is read out of the estate's bytes.

## What this record is, and what it is not

It is a map of the **dependency**. A row says ArWen ships a translation unit,
names the Python module that launches it, and reproduces what ArWen's own
physics registry demands before the option is selectable.

It is **not** a GM1 support claim. GM1 consumes exactly one ArWen component
today, WSM6 microphysics, through the seam recorded in `CM1-ARWEN-SEAM.md`.
A kernel listed here has no GM1 seam, no CM1 cadence binding, no CM1-side
carrier contract and no GM1 oracle unless a GM1 record says so.
`GM1-PHYSICS-SUPPORT.json` remains the GM1 admission ledger, and it is the
file that decides what a GM1 configuration may select.

The `maturity`, `scientific_evidence`, `reachability` and `warnings` fields
are reproduced verbatim from ArWen's registry. They are **ArWen's claims about
ArWen**, not GM1 measurements. Read them before wiring anything: most options
carry `maturity: implemented-unverified` and `scientific_evidence: none`, and
several carry warnings that name a known correctness bug rather than a parity
gap.

## What the 2.8.0 -> 2.8.9 bump made newly available

`newly_available_since_the_superseded_estate` is a mechanical two-estate diff,
not a changelog reading. A row means the bytes arrived; it says nothing about
whether the kernel is correct, whether ArWen validated it, or whether GM1 can
call it.

| Measure | 2.8.0 | 2.8.9 |
|---|---:|---:|
| CUDA translation units | 94 | 166 |
| `__global__` entry points | 611 | 951 |
| Physics-registry options | 40 | 48 |
| Shared device headers | 4 | 47 |
| Package files under `gpuwm/` | 1,429 | 2,197 |

Seventy-two translation units were added and none was removed. They include
the original Tiedtke cumulus scheme (`tiedtke`), the UW moist-turbulence PBL
(`uwpbl`), the FAST_SBM bin microphysics (`fast_sbm`), a generated classic
Thompson column unit (`thompson_column`), the CLM lake model (`lake`), the
urban canopy models (`urban_ucm`, `urban_bep`, `urban_bep_bem`), a Noah mosaic
unit, terrain drag, the chemistry and fire-spread families (`chem_*`,
`sfire_*`) and further RRTMG units.

Twenty-five existing units changed their entry points. Three of them lost
one or more, which is what can strand a caller: `acoustic` lost
`advance_mu_th_msf` and `advance_w_phi_msf`, `gf` lost `gf_gfdrv_stage`, and
`thompson` lost `thompson_frozen_vapor_cloud_network` while gaining nine. The
units GM1 launches (`wsm6`, `morrison` and `refl`) kept theirs.

Eight registry options were added and none was removed: `cumulus/tiedtke`,
`microphysics/fast-sbm-mp30`, `microphysics/tempo`, `pbl/uw` and four
`urban/*` rows. Twenty-six existing options changed at least one field, and
this time `microphysics/wsm6-mp6` and `microphysics/morrison-mp10` are both
among them (constraints, consumers and, for WSM6, parameters). Four vertical
bounds were added (FAST_SBM, TEMPO, the original Tiedtke and the UW PBL).
**No existing vertical bound changed value**, which the survey test asserts: a
moved bound would silently re-admit or re-refuse a geometry that a recorded
GM1 run already depends on.

This record counts bytes; it does not say whether a kernel's arithmetic
changed. For the units GM1 executes it did: `wsm6.cu` and `refl.cu` respell
constant divisions, and `morrison.cu` was restructured. The WSM6 and Morrison
executed surfaces are priced separately, field by field, by the estate-delta
audit that `tools/audit_arwen_wsm6_estate_delta.py` regenerates for 2.8.9
(`CM1-ARWEN-SEAM.md` quotes its numbers). Read that record, not this one,
before trusting either seam across the bump.

The 2.7.4 -> 2.8.0 bump added no translation unit and twelve Thompson entry
points, and removed one radiation option. The earlier 2.5.8 -> 2.7.4 bump is
where `ntiedtke` (New Tiedtke, `cu_physics = 16`), `milbrandt2_zet` and
`lbc_time` arrived, and where the Milbrandt-Yau, WDM6, P3 and New Tiedtke
vertical bounds were first declared.

## The four questions a wiring lane has to answer

For each component the survey answers these directly, so a lane does not
rediscover them:

1. **Which translation unit?** `kernels.<unit>.translation_unit` plus its
   SHA-256 in the pinned estate, its `entry_points` (the symbol names a
   launcher passes to `get_kernel(unit, symbol)`), its `shared_headers`, and
   its `compile_time_defines`: the `#ifndef` knobs a launcher can override
   through the int-define hook. That last one is how WSM6's per-thread column
   capacity is chosen (`WSM6_KMAX`), which is the whole mechanism behind
   `ARWEN-WSM6-CAPACITY-256-AUTHORITY.md`.
2. **Which Python driver?** `python_drivers` lists modules that name the unit
   as the literal first argument of a `get_kernel`/`load_module` call: a
   launch site, full stop. `python_modules_naming_it` lists modules that hold
   the name in a constant or a tuple they loop over, which is how the RRTMG,
   Thompson-aerosol, Noah-MP, `refl` and `nest` units are reached. A unit in
   the second list is not unreachable; it is reached indirectly.
   `sibling_module_public_functions` gives the callable surface of
   `gpuwm/core/<unit>.py` where one exists.
3. **What must be true before the option is selectable?**
   `registry_options.<component>/<option>.constraints` carries
   `requires_components` (with ArWen's own reason prose),
   `required_settings`, and `forbidden_setting_values`. These are real
   refusals with real reasons: the YSU row, for instance, requires the
   revised or classic MM5 surface layer because YSU divides by the similarity
   denominators only those two publish, and running it against any other
   surface layer produces finite, plausible, wrong values for the whole
   forecast. `asset_requirement_ids` names table sets that must be installed
   (Kain-Fritsch's lookup table, P3's lookup table, Thompson's ~370 MB table
   set, the RTE-RRTMGP netCDF tables).
4. **What state does it need?** `driver_state_attributes` is every
   `DomainState` attribute the unit's own launch sites touch, read by AST from
   the modules in `driver_state_attribute_sources`. An **empty** list is a real
   answer, not a gap: `ysu`, `noah` and `mynn_pbl` take explicit device arrays
   and never see the state object, so their binding lives in their caller,
   `gpuwm.core.physics`, whose own attribute set is in the same survey. Three
   vocabularies let a reader interpret a row: `domain_state_fields` (the 126
   carriers `gpuwm/core/state.py` binds, including the scheme-conditional
   moisture set it allocates through `setattr` loops), `domain_state_methods`
   (so `state.scratch(...)` is not mistaken for a carrier), and
   `driver_state_attributes_bound_outside_the_state_module` (the surface-layer
   exchange coefficients `ch`/`cm`/`fm`/`fh` and the ozone pair `moz`/`mozsgn`,
   which their own driver attaches to the state object; a GM1 seam that
   allocated only what `state.py` declares would hand those launchers a state
   missing exactly these). What must SURVIVE a call is a different question,
   and the registry answers it: `consumers.ring_guard.state_fields`.
   `local_frame_bytes` gives the measured per-thread local frame per compile
   platform, which is the number ArWen's own launch-time VRAM reservation is
   computed from: WSM6 reads 7,216 bytes at its 64-level tier on all six
   recorded platforms, the same number `ARWEN-WSM6-CAPACITY-256-AUTHORITY.md`
   measured offline.
5. **What geometry will it refuse?** `vertical_level_bounds` is
   `[minimum, maximum]` from `gpuwm/physics_vertical_contract.py`. A `null`
   ceiling means the port works on global `(ncol, nk)` workspaces and declares
   no per-thread column tier; the floor is still real and still refuses.
   `vertical_contract_constant` names the constant to read.

## Joining a kernel row to a registry option

The survey does **not** publish a kernel-to-option mapping, because ArWen has
none to read: `physics_registry_v2.json` names no translation unit, and only
microphysics dispatches through a literal selector table
(`gpuwm.core.microphysics._dispatch_scheme`). Every other component is
dispatched inside `gpuwm.core.physics`'s driver class against named constants.
A derived join would be a guess wearing a measurement's clothes, and the rows
it got wrong (fifteen `noahmp_*` units behind one `land_surface/noah-mp`
option; six `rrtmg*` units behind two radiation options) are exactly the ones a
lane would rely on.

Join by hand, in this order, all of it mechanical from rows the survey does
publish:

1. From the option: `registry_options.<component>/<option>.selectors` gives the
   namelist selector and value (`{"mp_physics": 6}`).
2. From the selector to the driver: the component's dispatch arm names the
   module. For microphysics that is one `elif cfg.mp_physics == N` chain; for
   the rest it is `gpuwm.core.physics`.
3. From the driver to the units: any kernel row whose `python_drivers` or
   `python_modules_naming_it` contains that module. A scheme normally owns
   `gpuwm/core/<unit>.py` beside `kernels/<unit>.cu`, which is why
   `sibling_module` is published.
4. From the units back to the geometry: `vertical_level_bounds` on the option,
   and `local_frame_bytes` on each unit.

## The bound that matters most to GM1's working lane

GM1's cartesian lane runs deep columns: 237 levels at 75 m. The survey's
`vertical_level_bounds` table is therefore the first thing to check for any
candidate component, because several are far shallower than the grid:

| Constant | Bounds | Note for a deep GM1 column |
|---|---|---|
| `WSM6_VERTICAL_LEVEL_BOUNDS` | `(2, 80)` | lifted to `(2, 256)` only by the GM1 capacity-256 overlay |
| `WDM6_VERTICAL_LEVEL_BOUNDS` | `(2, 80)` | same 80-level ceiling, no GM1 overlay exists |
| `YSU_VERTICAL_LEVEL_BOUNDS` | `(4, 128)` | refuses a 237-level column outright |
| `SHINHONG_VERTICAL_LEVEL_BOUNDS` | `(4, 128)` | refuses a 237-level column outright |
| `MYJ_VERTICAL_LEVEL_BOUNDS` | `(4, 128)` | refuses a 237-level column outright |
| `KF_VERTICAL_LEVEL_BOUNDS` | `(8, 128)` | refuses a 237-level column outright |
| `SASE_VERTICAL_LEVEL_BOUNDS` | `(2, 128)` | refuses a 237-level column outright |
| `MORRISON`/`THOMPSON`/`THOMPSON_AEROSOL` | `(2, 256)` | admits 237 natively |
| `NSSL2`/`MILBRANDT2` | `(3, 256)` | admits 237 natively |
| `MYNN`/`GF`/`NEW_TIEDTKE`/`TIEDTKE`/`UWPBL`/`FAST_SBM`/`P3` | floor only | no declared ceiling |
| `TEMPO_VERTICAL_LEVEL_BOUNDS` | `(3, 256)` | admits 237 natively |
| `ACOUSTIC_VERTICAL_LEVEL_BOUNDS` | `(1, 256)` | ArWen's own dycore bound, not a physics one |

A 128-level ceiling is a compile-time per-thread column tier, the same kind of
bound WSM6's is. Lifting one is the capacity-overlay pattern in
`ARWEN-WSM6-CAPACITY-256-AUTHORITY.md`: build a modified copy of the estate
from a byte-audited base, never edit the estate, pin both the base and the
result, and measure the stack frame the deeper tier costs before claiming it
runs.

## What GM1 lacks today, and where the estate has something for it

GM1's cartesian lane has CM1's `sgsmodel=1` prognostic-TKE closure and WSM6
microphysics. It has no radiation, PBL, cumulus, land surface, surface drag or
grid nesting. The estate ships candidates for every one of those: radiation
(`rrtmg_*`, `rrtmgp_*`), PBL (`ysu`, `mynn_pbl`, `myjpbl`, `shinhong`),
surface layer (`sfclay`, `myjsfc`, `mynn_surface`), land surface (`noah`,
`ruc`, the fifteen `noahmp_*` units), cumulus (`kf`, `gf`, `ntiedtke`),
nesting and lateral boundaries (`nest`, `nest_microphysics`, `spec_bdy`,
`lbc_*`, `openbc`), and diagnostics (`refl`, `uh_diag`, `vert_interp`).

Shipping a kernel is not the same as GM1 being able to call it. Every one of
those needs what WSM6 needed: a CM1-owned semantic adapter, a declared carrier
contract, a CM1 cadence binding, a measured oracle, and a seam record that
says plainly what it substitutes and what that costs. The survey shortens the
first day of that work. It does not do any of it.
